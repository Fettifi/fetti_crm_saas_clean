### Recommendation

**"Everyone's income is unusual up close."**

It is the only line that does what was asked. It keeps the whole point of the current hero — that
Fetti is built for income that does not fit a template — and inverts it so it INCLUDES rather than
selects. The W-2 nurse with overtime, the veteran with BAH, the retiree drawing down assets and the
investor with four doors are all unusual up close, and each reads it as being about them. It also
sets up the page beneath it: a register of income shapes rather than a menu of products.

Runner-up: **"One rulebook can only ever say no."** — sharper and more competitive; it sells the
broker model itself, and is colder.

### CLARITY / ANTI-ANXIETY — sell the answer, not the product.

**Big idea.** Fetti answers the only question that actually keeps people from asking — "where do I really stand?" — before the borrower spends a credit pull, a document hunt, or their pride; the product being sold is certainty, and the proof is that nothing is at risk while you get it.

Taglines:
- "Know where you stand. Then decide." — Replaces an income-shape claim with the universal emotional benefit. It says nothing about who you are, so nobody self-selects out: the salaried first-timer, the veteran, the retiree and the forty-door investor all want the same thing from that sentence. It also sequences the relationship correctly — information first, decision second, commitment last — which is the entire operating difference, and the second clause hands control back to the reader, which is precisely what an anxious borrower is afraid of losing.
  - leaves out: The borrower who already knows where they stand and is shopping on price or speed — the experienced DSCR investor comparing rates, the agent who needs a pre-approval letter tonight. It is silent on capability: it does not hint at bank-statement, DSCR or P&L qualifying, so it does none of the alt-doc differentiation the current line does well.
- "A straight answer in two minutes." — Puts the mechanism in the headline, so the promise is falsifiable rather than atmospheric. 'Straight' is the word borrowers use about lenders they trust and quietly indicts the runaround everyone expects; 'two minutes' prices the risk of asking at almost nothing. It is the strongest line for paid traffic because it reads as a complete offer with no decoding required.
  - leaves out: Genuinely complex files — foreign national, post-bankruptcy, 12-month P&L only — where two minutes cannot be the whole answer, so the sophisticated end can read it as thin lead-gen. It leaves out the investor who measures speed in days to close rather than minutes to answer, and it is a promise about us rather than a benefit to them.
- "The answer first. The paperwork after." — Names the inversion directly and is the most memorable line of the five — it sticks because it describes a reversed order the reader has personally suffered. Strong on-site as the turn or as the headline over 'What happens next', and it quietly handles the biggest objection (the document hunt) without ever mentioning documents.
  - leaves out: Full-doc salaried borrowers who do not find paperwork frightening and may hear looseness where we meant sequence. It risks implying documents will not be needed at all, which sets up a later disappointment, and it says nothing about jurisdiction, price or human contact.
- "Find out before it costs you anything." — Attacks the exact cost the visitor is tallying — credit score, time, pride — and makes inaction the expensive option. Best performing of the five as a button-adjacent or meta-description line because it is pure risk reversal, and it is the only one that speaks directly to the person who has been putting this off for a year.
  - leaves out: Invites tyre-kickers and says nothing about competence, programmes or footprint. - "Clarity before you commit to anything." — The most adult and most brand-durable of the five; it reads as a principle the firm operates by rather than a campaign, and it ages well across home, investment and business lending without a word changing.
